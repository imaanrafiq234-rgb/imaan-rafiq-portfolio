Professor Layton Device

World-building through form

Year
2024
Discipline
Concept exercise
Role
Visual DNA mapping · Form ideation · CAD · Physical prototype
Tools
SOLIDWORKS · Physical model · Reference analysis

Overview

A real-world, tactile interpretation of a handheld communication device inspired by Nintendo's Professor Layton — world-building through form.

Have you ever wondered what the technology from your favourite game worlds would feel like in the palm of your hand?

I absolutely adored playing this game growing up — the animation style, the storytelling and the whimsical mechanics completely captivated me. Getting to deconstruct its visual universe as a designer has been brilliant.

4Design pipeline stages
0Touchscreens — physical mechanics only
2D → 3DReference page to physical model

The visual DNA

Mapping the design language: I pulled form cues directly from the game's architecture and vehicles — turning arched windows into a speaker grille, and clockwork gears into functional interface selectors.

Form ideation

Sketching multiple asymmetrical oval silhouettes to see how a stylised, pebble-like chassis could comfortably drop into a real human palm.

The CAD breakdown

Moving the chosen form into 3D space to refine the physical proportions, mechanical gear tolerances, and clean up the overall envelope.

Interaction and prototype

Mapping the user journey and testing the final, physical piece. Fictional tech doesn't care about internal component constraints, but real hands do. Forcing a whimsical shape to perfectly balance its weight and button spacing for a natural, one-handed grip is a masterclass in ergonomics.

In a world dominated by flat glass touchscreens, this device relies entirely on physical mechanics. The side dials turn to select 'communicators', and the controls are purposefully spaced to encourage slow, deliberate engagement rather than rapid tapping. It turns device interaction into a puzzle-solving experience.

Fictional or not, great product design always comes down to asking: if this existed in real life, how would it feel to hold, click and use?
