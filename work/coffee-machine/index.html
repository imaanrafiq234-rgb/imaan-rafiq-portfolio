Form, flow and internal architecture

    • The design language draws heavily from a sleek, organic, flowy aesthetic — specifically inspired by the elegance and posture of a cobra. The goal was to merge fluid movement with the clean, minimalist lines expected in high-end kitchen appliances.

    The hidden challenge: internal layout

    While the exterior silhouette often gets all the spotlight, one crucial aspect of product design deserves far more recognition: the internal layout.

    Fitting pumps, heating elements, water reservoirs and tubing into a dynamic, tapered organic form is no easy feat. I am genuinely proud of how I tackled and sorted out the internal architecture to ensure functionality does not compromise form.

    Visual direction

    Visual direction is an important factor in order to fully understand and communicate the design. Looking at typical coffee machines, I wanted my design to reflect a strong connection to the home environment, focusing on simplicity and a sense of nature.

    To achieve this I incorporated tactility and warmth using soft edges and smooth forms, and referenced the origins of coffee by integrating elements inspired by coffee beans. A warm colour palette enhances the inviting, natural feel. The visual direction was influenced by designers such as Nendo and brands like Kikkoman, which emphasise minimalism and subtle elegance. For the drip tray I took inspiration from Pikka, ensuring the design is secure, stable and safe — with the machine accommodating a cup seamlessly as part of the overall design.

    Form analysis and layout study

    Side and back views were analysed to test two competing architectures. A tall layout suits a machine that is broad in a horizontal area rather than vertical; a wide layout suits a machine that is compact in a short vertical area rather than horizontal. Working both through on paper settled the internal layout before any CAD time was spent.

    Clay models and interface

    Modelling a product — physically, in clay — is a key part of fixing ergonomics and applying the user journey. I am glad to do this in every product I assess; without it there is no kaizen.

    Interface iterations improved access and user interaction, and the final design fully integrates the water tank and makes clear how the machine is operated.

    Outcome

    The final design gives the machine a cobra and hourglass look, which makes it unique while staying modern and natural — meeting the design analysis established by the form study. A selection of coffee options at the top of the machine broadens the target customer and keeps them satisfied within the design.
