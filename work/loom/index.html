    • I used to waste days trying to make initial designs perfect. Then I realised overthinking the first step just kills momentum.

    To sharpen my product design skills I created LOOM. While it is a fully functioning e-commerce site, it served as a sandbox to showcase my end-to-end UX/UI process — taking a rough concept and turning it into a user-tested interface.

    Mapping the flow

    The first slide is a low-fidelity six-step customer journey: browse, evaluate, add to basket, checkout, confirm and thank you. Plotting it rough and early meant friction points surfaced before any visual design had been committed to.

    High-fidelity UI

    From the flow I moved into a full interface: dynamic product grids, wishlist triggers, and clear category badges for Tops, Trousers, Outerwear and Knitwear. Product cards carry price, imagery and collection labels without crowding the grid.

    The brand voice leans editorial — 'Made for the long way around', 'A considered wardrobe and home, for wherever the day takes you' — so the layout gives typography room to breathe.

    Figma workflow

    The whole system was built with Auto Layout, component libraries and variants for total scalability. Every screen is assembled from reusable components, so new product categories or states can be introduced without redrawing the interface.
