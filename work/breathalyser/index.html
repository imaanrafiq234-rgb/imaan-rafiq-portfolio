Overview

A handheld alcohol breathalyser taken through the full pipeline — red-line concepts, parametric CAD and a high-fidelity render.

As part of my ongoing series sharing design and form analysis two to three times a week, this is the complete evolution of a handheld alcohol breathalyser: ideation, engineering and presentation.

28 mmHandle depth at the palm
15 mmMouthpiece axis offset
45°Maintained sight-line angle

From form to envelope

The loose volumes explored in the early sketch phase were locked down to a precise footprint in the CAD model — the absolute sweet spot for housing the internal fuel-cell sensor block while keeping the device highly portable.

From lines to ergonomics

The organic curves from the sketch became an engineered reality. The handle tapers down to a 28 mm depth, creating a natural contour that sits securely in the user's palm.

Preserving the sight-line

To ensure a user can clearly read the screen while blowing into the unit, the mouthpiece axis was engineered with a specific 15 mm offset from the top casing. This perfectly maintains the 45° sight-line angle down to the display screen.

Ready for production

In the final render you can see how the UI elements come together. The screen pocket is optimised for a crisp, standard OLED display, paired with tactically spaced buttons to prevent mispresses.

Taking a product all the way from a blank page to a realistic render is a masterclass in compromise — finding where styling, engineering metrics and user interaction meet seamlessly.
