Two skill sets, one designer

Physical sketching is the tactile freedom of pen and paper, where quick ideation and raw creativity happen. Digital layouts and CAD are the precision toolsets required to translate those ideas into manufacturable reality.

As modern designers, it's no longer enough to know just one or the other. We need to be fluid in both mediums to effectively communicate our intent.

A robust sketch is built, not drawn

It follows a strict hierarchy of operations. Phase one, foundation: sketch plane, centreline, construction line. Phase two, generation: line, arc, circle, ellipse, spline, polygon. Phase three, modification: offset, trim, extend, mirror, pattern. Phase four, control: constraint and dimension.

CAD problems and solutions

A working reference for the recurring problems a CAD designer faces and how to answer them.

  • Frequent design changes from customers — prepare a detailed design brief and get approval before starting the final design.
  • Incorrect measurements and dimensions — verify all measurements and follow standard size and manufacturing guidelines.
  • Designs difficult to manufacture — coordinate with the production team and design to manufacturing capability.
  • Tight project deadlines — use time management techniques, CAD templates and design libraries to save time.
  • Lack of software expertise — attend training and practise advanced tools.
  • Slow computers and software crashes — upgrade hardware and optimise files.
  • Communication gaps between departments — regular meetings and clear documentation.
  • File management and version control — naming conventions, backups and cloud storage.
  • Difficulty creating realistic renders — learn advanced rendering techniques and use high-performance systems.
  • Keeping up with industry trends — continuous learning and online courses.
