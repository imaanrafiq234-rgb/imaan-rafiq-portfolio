    • Back to basics: why I still can't give up my sketchbook. For this week's design snippet I'm closing down the CAD software, putting the digital tablet away, and going right back to where every decent product starts — just a pen and a bit of paper.

    It stops you marrying your first idea

    If I jump straight into 3D software, I instinctively start polishing the very first thing I build. A sketchbook forces me to churn out ideas fast — testing tall and bulky shapes, straight-sided barrels, or softer, fluid lines in a matter of minutes.

    It catches the messy thoughts

    The little notes in the margins — 'taller/bulky?', 'flowy', 'grip?' — are basically me arguing with myself in real time. It's a quick, permanent log of what I was thinking before getting lost in pixels.

    It trains your eye like nothing else

    Doing quick 3D ellipses and shading by hand forces you to really understand proportions. If you can make a cylinder look right on paper, you can model it properly in CAD. With all the incredible digital tools and AI renderers out there today, it's easy to feel like physical sketching is a bit old-school — but for me, keeping up this tangible, pen-on-paper habit is a massive part of my process.
