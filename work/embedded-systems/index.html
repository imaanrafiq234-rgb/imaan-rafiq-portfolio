Overview

A beginner's guide to embedded systems — block diagrams, component comparisons and applications, written and drawn to be genuinely readable.

A specialised computer system designed to perform a specific task inside a larger device. This series breaks embedded systems down for people meeting them for the first time.

Key characteristics

Embedded systems share a set of defining traits: dedicated function, real-time operation, low power consumption, reliable performance and compact size.

The basic block diagram runs from input devices through a microcontroller with processor and memory, out to output devices — a switch and temperature sensor in, a fan or buzzer out.

Working principle

Sensor, input processing, decision making, output activation. The worked example is a temperature sensor feeding a microcontroller that switches a fan on. The core advantages are fast response, low power consumption, high reliability, compact size and real-time performance.

Platforms compared

Arduino Uno is a basic, easy-to-use microcontroller great for learning electronics. The ESP32 is a powerful, compact microcontroller with wireless communication across home and industrial applications. STM32 offers control-intensive communication components. Raspberry Pi is a single-board computer capable of master-level tasks.

Applications and future

Embedded systems sit inside industrial automation, medical devices, washing machines, AC units, robots, smart meters, IoT and smart home products, aerospace navigation systems and drones.

The future runs through Industry 4.0, industrial robotics, artificial intelligence, the Internet of Things, autonomous vehicles and smart factories — with embedded engineering at the centre of each.
